Planning for Potential Outcomes

An interactive artifact set accompanying the Pedagogical Friction dissertation thought experiment — data use, dissemination, interventions, ecological systems, and contingent interpretation.

Proposal stage. Everything here is prospective. No participant data have been collected, and the study reports no findings. These tools model what would happen with findings; they are not study instruments and they collect nothing.

What this set demonstrates

Each tool turns a section of the written thought experiment into something a reader can do rather than only read. Together they model the study’s central commitment: knowledge produced with a community should return to that community in usable form. The tools are static, dependency-free HTML/CSS/JS pages designed for GitHub Pages, consistent with the dissertation’s instrument architecture (no server, no database, no data collection).

Companion to: Planning for Potential Outcomes: A Thought Experiment on Data Use, Dissemination, and Contingent Interpretation in the Pedagogical Friction Study (Miner, 2026).

Design principles

No data collection. Every tool runs entirely in the browser; nothing is stored or transmitted, mirroring the IRB posture of the dissertation’s instrument suite. Framework-honest. The tools present the Pedagogical Friction Framework as an empirical question, not a settled conclusion — the Scenario Explorer exists precisely to show what happens when the data push back. Practitioner-first language. Framework vocabulary appears alongside ordinary professional terms (the head, the room, the world, the system) so the tools are usable without the theoretical apparatus.
