Stage 1 — What the data becomes

Raw evidence collection

Educator and leader interviews, card sort placements and reasoning, institutional documents, branched survey responses (~200 educators), retrospective university-student accounts, and structural context from NCES and RAND panels. De-identified before any analysis support; participant responses stay local to the browser in the instrument suite and move only through IRB-approved secure upload.
▼

Integrated findings analysis

Two-cycle thematic analysis with friction and threat code layers; descriptive and exploratory quantitative analysis; integration through joint displays recording convergence, divergence, expansion, and silence by research question. Divergence is retained as evidence, not resolved away.
▼

Refined framework testing

The Pedagogical Friction Framework enters as theory and exits corrected by practitioner evidence — dimensions confirmed, merged, renamed, or reordered according to what participants actually describe. The framework’s utility is an empirical question, not a commitment the data must confirm.
▼

Translated artifacts usable

Plain-language criteria for distinguishing access support from cognitive bypass; assessment design patterns that reconnect product to process; policy language addressing learning science, not only integrity; PD module outlines; the friction-audit routine demonstrated in this artifact set. The community should not have to read a dissertation to benefit from one.

Stage 2 — Four return tracks

Participant track

Audience
Interview, card sort, and survey participants
Artifacts
Member-checked theme summaries during analysis; plain-language executive summary at completion
Channel
Direct return + public dissertation hub
Why
Reciprocity — recruitment through professional networks creates an obligation to return knowledge first to those who produced it

Scholarly track

Audience
Researchers in media ecology, learning sciences, AI-in-education
Artifacts
Dissertation; findings-stage article extending Miner (2026b); practitioner-adjacent journal submissions
Channel
NLU Digital Commons, peer-reviewed venues
Why
Moves the framework from conceptual argument to empirically examined contribution

Professional track

Audience
EdTech leaders, CTOs, instructional coaches nationally
Artifacts
Conference sessions, webinars, committee briefings, articles for member publications
Channel
ISTE, CoSN, state affiliates (IETL, ICE), regional consortia — the same networks that produced the sample
Why
Findings flow back through the channels that produced the data; the community is national because the condition is

Local / district track

Audience
Districts, boards, building leaders, teacher teams
Artifacts
PD modules sequenced by friction dimension; policy review templates; the friction-audit protocol; family-facing language about productive struggle
Channel
Direct district adoption, consulting, open templates on the hub sites
Why
The exosystem is the leverage point — these artifacts target exactly the policy, PD, and assessment-governance layer the findings identify

The governing principle

Dissemination is layered to match how this community actually consumes knowledge. A superintendent will read a two-page policy brief; a teacher will use an audit routine; a researcher will read the methods chapter. Same findings, four registers — and the participants hear them first.
